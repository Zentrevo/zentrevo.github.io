Is Travel Insurance Worth It for Short Trips?

A clear, honest breakdown of when travel insurance actually pays off on a 3-5 day trip — and when you can reasonably skip it.

Short answer: it depends more on what you're protecting against than how many days you're traveling. A 3-day trip with a fully refundable flight and hotel needs different coverage than a 3-day trip with a non-refundable package and a connecting flight.

When Travel Insurance Actually Pays Off on Short Trips

01

Non-Refundable Bookings

If your flights or hotel are non-refundable and cost more than a policy would, trip cancellation coverage is usually worth it — even for a weekend trip.

Worth it: Usually yes
02

International Trips, Even Short Ones

Your domestic health insurance likely doesn't cover you abroad. A medical emergency on a 3-day international trip can cost more than the entire trip itself.

Worth it: Strongly yes
03

Tight Connections or Multiple Flights

Short trips with tight layovers carry real risk of missed connections eating into your limited time — some policies cover rebooking costs.

Worth it: Often yes
04

Fully Refundable, Domestic, Low-Risk

If everything's refundable and you're staying within your home country's healthcare coverage, a policy may genuinely add little value.

Worth it: Often skippable

The Math Most People Skip

A basic short-trip policy often runs 4-8% of total trip cost. If your trip costs $600 and the policy is $30, the real question isn't "is $30 worth it" — it's "what's the actual probability I'd lose more than $30 without it, and would I be able to absorb that loss comfortably." For most people on a tight budget, that math favors buying coverage on anything non-refundable.

Questions to Actually Ask Before Skipping Coverage

  • Is any part of this trip non-refundable? If yes, cancellation coverage likely pays for itself
  • Am I leaving my home country? If yes, check what your existing health coverage actually excludes abroad
  • Does my credit card already include some trip protection? Many premium cards do — check the fine print before double-paying
  • Would I be genuinely fine absorbing the full cost of a missed flight or canceled hotel?
  • Check the CDC Travelers' Health page for your destination — higher-risk destinations tip the math further toward buying coverage

"The trips that need insurance most are rarely the long, expensive ones people assume. They're the short, non-refundable ones nobody thinks to protect."

Credit Card Coverage: Check This First

Before buying a standalone policy, check whether a credit card used to book the trip already includes trip cancellation or interruption coverage — many premium travel cards do, automatically, at no extra cost. It's often incomplete compared to a dedicated policy, but it may be enough for a short, low-risk trip.
