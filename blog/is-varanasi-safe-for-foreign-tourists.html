Is Varanasi Safe for Foreign Tourists?

An honest, practical guide to scams, women's safety, transport, the ghats, food and etiquette, so you can go prepared.

Varanasi is one of the oldest living cities in the world, and one of the most intense. Cremation fires burn beside the river, cows share narrow lanes with motorbikes, and a boatman may call out to you every few steps. It is easy to see why first-time visitors ask whether it is safe.

This guide gives you a straight answer, then the practical detail: the scams people actually run into, how to move around, what to know about the ghats, food and water, women's safety and local etiquette.

The short answer

Varanasi is visited safely by large numbers of foreign tourists. The realistic risks are scams, pickpocketing in crowds, disorientation in the old lanes and stomach upsets, not random violence. That said, no city is risk-free, and official advisories for India ask travelers to stay alert. Prepare, stay aware and you are likely to have a memorable trip.

What official advisories say

The US State Department currently rates India at Level 2, "exercise increased caution", citing crime and terrorism, with some areas carrying higher risk. Its highest-risk areas are places such as Jammu and Kashmir, the India-Pakistan border area, parts of central and eastern India and Manipur. Varanasi, in Uttar Pradesh, is not among the regions it singles out. The advisory also notes that violent crime, including sexual assault, has occurred at tourist sites in India.

Take that seriously but keep it in proportion. It describes India as a whole, and advisories change. Check your own government's current page before you book, such as the US State Department or the UK's FCDO, and again just before you fly. Links are in the official sources list below.

The real risks, in plain terms

Most problems travelers report in Varanasi are about friction and money rather than danger:

  • Scams and touts around the station, ghats and temples
  • Pickpocketing in dense crowds, especially at the evening aarti and in markets
  • Getting lost in the old city, where lanes are narrow, uneven and poorly lit at night
  • Animals: cows and bulls in lanes, and monkeys that grab bags, food and sunglasses
  • Stomach upsets from water or food you are not used to
  • High water and slippery steps on the ghats, especially in the monsoon

Common scams in Varanasi and how to avoid them

None of these are unique to Varanasi, but they are common enough to be worth knowing in advance.

Boat rides at inflated prices

Boat fares are usually negotiated rather than fixed. Agree the price, the duration and the route before you step on, ask what is included, and pay at the end. Your hotel or a trusted guide can tell you a fair range for the season.

Taxi and rickshaw overcharging

Drivers at stations and the airport may quote high fares or claim your hotel is closed or full to redirect you. Fix the fare before you set off, use a pre-booked pickup where possible, and confirm your hotel by phone yourself.

"Blessing" or fake priest scams

Around busy temples and ghats, someone may offer a blessing, a flower or a thread and then demand a large donation. You can politely decline and keep walking. Genuine offerings are made at temples where you choose to give.

Cremation ghat "donations" and photo demands

At the burning ghats, people may approach you claiming wood donations are needed or demanding money for photos. The families, not tourists, arrange the actual cremation costs. Do not photograph the cremations, and walk away from anyone pressuring you for money.

Guides and drivers steering you to shops

A friendly offer to show you a silk workshop or "special" market often comes with a commission. You are never obliged to buy. Say upfront that you only want to go where you have planned.

Bhang lassi

Bhang is a cannabis-based drink sold in some places, and its effects can be strong and unpredictable. It is wise to avoid it, especially when you are traveling alone or in an unfamiliar city.

Women's safety and solo travel

Many women visit Varanasi alone or in small groups. It can be rewarding, but expect more attention than at home and plan around it.

  • Dress modestly: cover shoulders and knees, and consider a light scarf. It helps you blend in and draws less attention.
  • Choose accommodation with strong reviews from solo women and a staffed front desk.
  • Arrange your arrival and any late-evening transfers in advance.
  • Avoid empty lanes and riverside stretches after dark; stay where other people are around.
  • Keep your hotel address and a local contact saved on your phone, and share your location with someone you trust.
  • Use women's compartments and women-only queues where available on trains.
  • Be firm and brief with unwanted attention, move toward other people and leave the situation if you feel uneasy.

The national women's helpline is 1091. Official advisories also publish guidance for women travelers, so read your government's version before you travel.

Is Varanasi safe at night?

Busy ghats and main roads are generally fine in the evening, particularly during the Ganga aarti when crowds gather. The quieter alleys of the old city are a different matter. They are dimly lit, confusing and easy to get lost in, and they empty out later at night.

  • Carry a phone with a charged battery and a small torch
  • Save your hotel's location offline
  • Plan your route back before it gets late
  • Hold bags in front of you in the aarti crowd

Getting around safely

Many old-city lanes are too narrow for cars, so you will walk a lot. For longer trips, a few habits go a long way:

  • Arrange an airport or station pickup through your hotel or tour operator, so you do not negotiate with touts after a long journey.
  • Agree fares before you go when using auto-rickshaws and e-rickshaws.
  • Avoid arriving with a heavily loaded pack on display, which marks you as a target; use a smaller bag for the day.
  • For day trips such as Sarnath, a private driver is simpler than negotiating each leg. Our guide to a private tour with a driver in India explains how it works.

The ghats, the river and the monsoon

The ghats are the heart of Varanasi, and they are safe enough to enjoy with a little care. Steps are worn and often wet, so wear shoes with grip and watch your footing in the dark. Crowds at Dashashwamedh Ghat are densest during the evening aarti, so arrive early and keep valuables secure.

Boat rides

A sunrise boat trip is one of the best things to do in the city. Choose a boat with life jackets, stay seated and keep your hands out of the water. Agree the price and timing first.

High water

The Ganges can rise sharply during the monsoon. In late August and early September 2026, floodwater submerged the ghats, and the district administration suspended boat operations as a precaution. If you travel in monsoon months, ask your hotel for current conditions, keep plans flexible and do not walk through flooded stretches near the river.

Cremation ghats

Manikarnika and Harishchandra are working cremation grounds. Observe quietly from a respectful distance, do not take photographs and ignore anyone demanding payment.

Swimming and bathing

Pilgrims bathe in the river as an act of faith. Visitors should not swim or bathe in it, and the water is not safe to drink.

Food and water precautions

  • Drink sealed bottled water, and check the seal is intact. Avoid tap water and ice of unknown origin.
  • Choose busy places with high turnover, and eat food that is freshly cooked and served hot.
  • Peel fruit yourself and be cautious with raw salads, especially in your first days.
  • Ease into street food rather than starting with the most adventurous stall.
  • Carry oral rehydration salts and any personal medication, and have travel insurance that covers medical care.
  • See a doctor or travel clinic well before departure for vaccine advice; the CDC publishes India health guidance.

Cultural etiquette that earns goodwill

  • Dress conservatively, especially at temples and on the ghats.
  • Remove shoes before entering temples and follow staff instructions about phones, bags and cameras, as rules can change.
  • Ask before photographing people, and never photograph cremations.
  • Greet with a polite "Namaste" and use your right hand to give and receive items.
  • Do not touch sacred objects or step over offerings.
  • Move calmly around cows and monkeys, and avoid showing food openly.

Emergency numbers to save before you land

112National emergency number
1363Tourist helpline, 24/7, multilingual (also 1800-11-1363)
1091Women's helpline

India's Ministry of Tourism runs the 1363 helpline around the clock. It offers information and advice, and can alert the relevant authorities if you are in difficulty. For anything urgent, call 112 first. Also save the contact details of your embassy or consulate, and keep a copy of your passport and visa separate from the originals.

Frequently asked questions

Is Varanasi safe for foreign tourists?

For most visitors, yes, provided you take sensible precautions. The most common problems are scams, petty theft in crowds, overwhelming touts and stomach upsets rather than violent crime. Official advisories still ask visitors to stay alert across India, so read your own government's advice before you go.

Is Varanasi safe for solo female travelers?

Many women travel here alone and have a good time, but it takes more planning than in many cities. Dress modestly, avoid empty lanes after dark, pre-arrange arrivals and late rides, and stay somewhere with good reviews from solo women. Trust your instincts and leave any situation that feels wrong.

Is Varanasi safe at night?

Busy ghats and main roads are generally fine in the evening, especially around the Ganga aarti. The narrow lanes of the old city are harder to navigate in the dark and quieter late at night. Carry a phone light, keep your hotel's address saved offline, and take a pre-arranged ride back.

Can I drink the water or bathe in the Ganges?

No. The river is sacred, but it is not safe to drink and tourists should avoid entering it. Drink sealed bottled water or water you know has been properly filtered.

Are boat rides on the Ganges safe?

Sunrise boat rides are a highlight, and many visitors do them without trouble. Agree the price and duration before boarding, ask for a life jacket, and stay seated. During high-water periods in the monsoon, authorities can suspend boat operations, so check locally.

What is the best time to visit Varanasi?

The cooler months, roughly October to March, are the most comfortable. The monsoon can bring high water, flooded ghats and suspended boat rides, as seen in late August and early September 2026.

Do I need a guide in Varanasi?

You don't strictly need one, but a trusted local guide makes the old city much easier and helps you avoid touts and commission traps. Choose guides through your hotel or an agency you trust, and agree fees up front.
