Best India Itinerary for 10 Days

A realistic route for first-time foreign travelers — Delhi, Agra, Jaipur, and Varanasi, with pacing that actually holds up.

Ten days is a good amount of time for a first India trip — enough to see more than just the Golden Triangle, but not so long that you need to plan an entire subcontinent-spanning route. The mistake most first-time travelers make with 10 days is trying to cover too much ground. India is big, distances between regions are real, and a rushed itinerary tends to leave you more tired than inspired.

Here's a realistic 10-day route covering Delhi, Agra, Jaipur, and Varanasi — four destinations, not eight — with enough time in each to actually experience it.

Why a Focused Route Beats a Packed One

It's tempting to look at a map of India and want to fit in the Golden Triangle, Rajasthan, Kerala, and Varanasi all in ten days. In practice, that usually means one or two rushed days in each place, most of your energy going into transit, and very little time to actually slow down anywhere.

Four destinations in ten days gives you two to three nights in most places — enough time to see the major sites without racing through them, and enough slack in the schedule to absorb a delayed flight or a day that just runs long. This is the pacing philosophy we use when building custom itineraries at Zentrevo: fewer places, done properly, almost always beats more places done in a hurry.

The 10-Day Itinerary

Day 1: Arrive in Delhi

Most international flights land in the evening or overnight. Plan for a quiet first day — settle in, adjust to the time difference, and save the sightseeing for tomorrow when you're not running on jet lag.

Day 2: Delhi

Split the day between Old and New Delhi. In Old Delhi, the Red Fort and Jama Masjid give you a sense of Mughal-era history, and a walk (or rickshaw ride) through Chandni Chowk's markets is a good introduction to the pace of Indian city life. In New Delhi, Humayun's Tomb and India Gate cover a different, more spread-out side of the city. A private guide is genuinely useful on this day — Old Delhi in particular can feel overwhelming to navigate solo on your first day in the country.

Day 3: Delhi to Agra

A private car makes this drive straightforward, usually taking around three to four hours depending on traffic. Arrive in Agra by early afternoon, visit Agra Fort, and see the Taj Mahal at sunset if timing allows — it's noticeably quieter than the morning crowds.

Day 4: Agra to Jaipur (via Fatehpur Sikri)

Start early for a sunrise visit to the Taj Mahal — this is genuinely worth the early alarm, since the light and the smaller crowds make a real difference. Then drive to Jaipur, stopping at Fatehpur Sikri along the way, a well-preserved Mughal-era city that's easy to visit as a roadside stop rather than a full detour.

Day 5: Jaipur

A full day for Amber Fort, the City Palace, and Jaipur's old city, with its pink-hued streets and bazaars. This is a good day for a local guide who can walk you through the history at each site rather than just the highlights.

Day 6: Jaipur (Slower Pace or Short Excursion)

Use this day as a buffer rather than packing in more sites. Some travelers spend it shopping for textiles and jewelry in Jaipur's markets; others take a shorter day trip nearby. Either way, resist the urge to fill it — this is where a 10-day trip either feels comfortable or starts to feel rushed, and a light day here makes a real difference to how the rest of the trip feels.

Day 7: Jaipur to Varanasi

This leg is best done by air rather than road or rail — the distance is significant, and a direct flight (typically via Delhi, depending on availability) is far more practical than a long train journey. Arrive in Varanasi in the afternoon or evening, settle in, and get an early night ahead of tomorrow's sunrise.

Day 8: Varanasi

Varanasi runs on its own rhythm. A sunrise boat ride on the Ganges is one of the more memorable mornings on this whole itinerary — the ghats are at their quietest and most atmospheric early in the day. Spend the rest of the day exploring the old city on foot, and return to the ghats in the evening for the aarti ceremony, when the riverside comes alive with lamps, chanting, and music.

Day 9: Varanasi (Second Morning), Then Fly Back to Delhi

A second, more relaxed morning in Varanasi — maybe a quieter walk through the old city, or simply more time at the ghats without the schedule of the first day. Fly back to Delhi in the afternoon, ready for departure the next day.

Day 10: Departure

Depending on your flight time, use the morning for any last errands or a final walk around your Delhi neighborhood before heading to the airport.

Travel Time Between Destinations

Roughly, What to Expect

  • Delhi to Agra: 3-4 hours by private car
  • Agra to Jaipur (via Fatehpur Sikri): 5-6 hours by private car, including the stop
  • Jaipur to Varanasi: best done by air; a direct road or rail route isn't practical for this leg given the distance
  • Varanasi to Delhi: best done by air for the same reason

Private Car vs Train or Flight on This Route

The Delhi-Agra-Jaipur leg works well by private car — the roads are well-traveled, the distances are manageable in a single day each, and a car lets you stop at Fatehpur Sikri along the way, which a train simply skips. Some travelers do Delhi-Agra by train instead, since that specific route has good rail connectivity, but Agra-Jaipur is less well served directly, which is where a private car tends to make more sense.

Jaipur to Varanasi and back to Delhi are a different story — the distance is too significant for road travel to make sense within a 10-day trip, so flights are the practical choice for both legs.

Where to Slow Down, and Where Not to Overpack

The two places worth resisting the urge to overfill are Jaipur and Varanasi. Jaipur has enough sites to genuinely fill a rushed single day, but a second, slower day (Day 6 above) is what makes the trip feel comfortable rather than frantic. Varanasi rewards a slower pace even more — its appeal is largely atmospheric, and trying to "see everything" in one day misses a lot of what makes it worth visiting in the first place.

Where people most often overpack a 10-day itinerary is by trying to add a fifth destination — Kerala, Goa, or a wildlife safari — on top of this route. Any of those are genuinely worth visiting, but not squeezed into the same ten days as Delhi, Agra, Jaipur, and Varanasi. They're better suited to a longer trip, or a separate one.

Practical Tips for Foreign Travelers

Worth Knowing Before You Go

  • Book domestic flights early — the Jaipur-Varanasi and Varanasi-Delhi legs rely on flights, and popular routes and times can fill up, especially during peak season
  • Build in a buffer day — Day 6 in this itinerary is intentionally light, so a delayed flight or a tired day doesn't derail the rest of the trip
  • Ease into the food — stick to busy, high-turnover restaurants before branching into more street food, especially in the first few days in Delhi
  • Dress modestly at religious sites, particularly in Varanasi, where a lot of the experience centers on the ghats and temples
  • A private guide in Delhi and Jaipur makes a genuine difference to how much you get out of the more historically dense sites

Best Time for This Itinerary

October through March is the most comfortable window for this route — cooler, clearer weather across Delhi, Agra, Jaipur, and Varanasi. April through June gets very hot, particularly in Rajasthan, and the monsoon (roughly July to September) brings heavy rain to parts of the region, which can affect both comfort and visibility at outdoor sites like Amber Fort.

Trip Planning Considerations

A few things worth thinking through before finalizing dates: whether your flight times allow for a genuinely restful first day, whether you want the sunset or sunrise Taj Mahal visit (or both, if your schedule allows), and how firm your return flight is, given that the Varanasi-Delhi leg depends on domestic flight availability. None of these are dealbreakers, but they're worth factoring in early rather than adjusting last minute.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is 10 days enough for a first trip to India?

Yes, for a focused route like this one. Ten days comfortably covers Delhi, Agra, Jaipur, and Varanasi without feeling rushed, as long as you resist adding extra destinations.

Should I visit Varanasi on a first India trip, or save it for later?

It works well on a first trip if you're genuinely curious about India's spiritual side. If you'd rather keep the itinerary purely historical, an extra day or two in Rajasthan (Udaipur, for example) is a reasonable substitute.

Can I add Kerala or Goa to a 10-day itinerary?

Not comfortably alongside Delhi, Agra, Jaipur, and Varanasi — the distances involved would turn most of the trip into transit. Either is better suited to a separate trip or a longer itinerary.

Should I take a private car or train between Delhi, Agra, and Jaipur?

A private car works well for this leg, especially since it allows a stop at Fatehpur Sikri. Some travelers do the Delhi-Agra segment by train, but Agra-Jaipur has weaker rail connectivity, so a car is usually more practical there.

Is a private guide necessary for this itinerary?

Not strictly necessary, but genuinely useful in Delhi and Jaipur, where the historical sites have a lot of context that's easy to miss without one.

What's the best time of year for this specific route?

October through March, when temperatures are cooler and skies are clearer across all four destinations.

Can this itinerary be adjusted for a shorter or longer trip?

Yes. Dropping Varanasi brings it closer to a 6-7 day Golden Triangle trip, and extending into Rajasthan beyond Jaipur (Udaipur or Jodhpur) works well if you have more than ten days available.
