Best National Parks to Visit in 2026

From Yellowstone's geysers to Torres del Paine's granite towers — the parks worth the permit hassle this year.

National parks remain one of the best values in travel — genuinely spectacular landscapes protected specifically so they stay that way, often for a fraction of the cost of comparable private tours. The trick in 2026 is navigating permits and crowds, which have both intensified at the most famous parks.

Top National Parks for 2026

01

Yellowstone, USA

Geothermal features found nowhere else on this scale, plus some of the best wildlife viewing in the continental United States.

Best: May–Sep
02

Torres del Paine, Chile

Patagonia's signature granite towers, with the W Trek offering one of the world's best multi-day hikes for reasonably fit travelers.

Best: Nov–Mar
03

Kruger National Park, South Africa

Africa's most accessible Big Five park, with excellent self-drive infrastructure alongside guided game drive options.

Best: May–Sep
04

Vatnajökull, Iceland

Europe's largest glacier by volume, with ice caves, volcanic terrain, and some of the most alien landscapes on the continent.

Best: Jun–Aug (summer access)

Permits: The Part Everyone Underestimates

Popular US parks now require timed-entry reservations during peak season, and permits for iconic hikes (Half Dome in Yosemite, certain Torres del Paine campsites) sell out months ahead. Check each park's official site directly rather than third-party booking sites, since permit systems change year to year.

National Park Planning Essentials

  • Check the official National Park Service site for current entry, permit, and timed-reservation requirements
  • Book accommodation inside or near the park 6+ months ahead for peak summer season
  • Visit at dawn or arrive the night before for the most popular short hikes — crowds build fast by mid-morning
  • Pack layers regardless of season — most parks span significant elevation changes
  • Download offline maps; cell coverage inside most parks is minimal to nonexistent

"A national park at 6am and the same park at noon are almost different places entirely."

Shoulder Season: The Underrated Strategy

Late spring and early fall consistently deliver the best ratio of good weather to manageable crowds across most major parks, often at meaningfully lower accommodation rates than peak summer. The tradeoff is slightly less predictable weather — a fair trade for most travelers given the crowd difference.
