What Currency to Use in Switzerland

Why Switzerland still uses its own currency, whether euros work anywhere, and how to handle money without overpaying.

Switzerland is surrounded by eurozone countries but isn't part of the EU, and it kept its own currency: the Swiss franc (CHF). It's one of the more common points of confusion for first-time visitors, especially coming from a multi-country European trip where euros have worked everywhere else.

Why Switzerland Doesn't Use the Euro

Not an EU Member

Switzerland has consistently opted out of EU membership through several referendums over the decades, which means it was never obligated to adopt the euro the way member states are. The Swiss franc has a long history as one of the world's more stable currencies, and that stability is part of why Switzerland has kept it rather than switching.

The Franc Is Considered a Safe-Haven Currency

Financially, the Swiss franc is often treated as a "safe haven" during global economic uncertainty, meaning it tends to hold or gain value when other currencies wobble. This matters more to economists than travelers, but it partly explains why Switzerland has little incentive to switch.

Can You Pay in Euros Anyway?

Border Towns and Tourist Areas

Some shops, hotels, and restaurants near the French, German, and Italian borders — and in heavily touristed spots like Zermatt or Interlaken — will accept euros. But they set their own exchange rate, which is usually worse than what you'd get from a bank or card.

Change Will Come Back in Francs

Even where euros are accepted, change is typically given in Swiss francs, meaning you'll end up needing francs eventually anyway. It's rarely worth relying on euro acceptance as a primary payment strategy.

Practical Money Tips for Switzerland

  • Use a debit/credit card with no foreign transaction fees for most purchases — cards are widely accepted, including at small shops
  • Withdraw francs from an ATM on arrival for small cash needs like transit tickets or mountain huts that don't take cards
  • Avoid airport currency exchange counters — rates are consistently worse than ATMs or city bank branches
  • Some mountain restaurants and rural spots are cash-preferred even if they technically accept cards, so keep some francs on hand outside cities

"Bring a card, get francs from an ATM on day one, and stop thinking about currency for the rest of the trip."

Is Switzerland Expensive Regardless of Currency?

Yes — Switzerland is consistently one of the more expensive countries in Europe regardless of which currency you're paying in. If you're planning a broader multi-country European trip, it's worth budgeting Switzerland separately rather than averaging costs across the whole itinerary, since prices there run noticeably higher than most neighboring countries.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Switzerland use the euro?

No. Switzerland uses the Swiss franc (CHF) and is not part of the eurozone or the EU, despite being surrounded by EU countries.

Can I pay in euros in Switzerland?

Some tourist areas and border towns accept euros, but at a less favorable exchange rate, and change is usually given in Swiss francs.

Is it better to use cash or card in Switzerland?

Cards are widely accepted, including at most small shops, and generally offer better exchange rates than cash. Keep some francs on hand for rural areas or mountain huts.

Where's the best place to exchange money for Switzerland?

ATM withdrawals in francs typically beat airport currency exchange counters and pre-ordering cash from your home bank.
