Do You Need a Visa to Visit Vietnam?

A clear breakdown of Vietnam's e-visa system, which countries get exemptions, and how long you can actually stay.

For most travelers, yes — Vietnam does require some form of visa, though the process has gotten considerably easier in recent years thanks to the e-visa system. A small number of nationalities get visa-free entry for short stays, but it's not the default for most passport holders.

Vietnam's E-Visa System

Since expanding its e-visa program, Vietnam now offers electronic visas to citizens of most countries, valid for stays of up to 90 days with single or multiple entry options. The process is entirely online: submit an application and passport photo, pay the fee, and receive an approval letter by email, typically within a few business days.

E-Visa Application Basics

  • Apply only through Vietnam's official government portal — third-party sites often charge inflated "service fees"
  • You'll need a passport valid for at least 6 months beyond your entry date and a digital passport photo
  • Processing typically takes 3-5 business days, though rush processing is sometimes available for a higher fee
  • Print your approval letter — border officials generally expect a physical copy, not just a phone screenshot

Which Countries Get Visa-Free Entry

A limited group of countries — including several within ASEAN and a handful of others with bilateral agreements — receive visa-free entry to Vietnam for short stays, typically capped at 15-45 days depending on the country and current policy. These exemption lists and durations do shift periodically, so it's worth confirming your specific country's status close to your travel date rather than relying on older information.

What Happens If You Overstay

Overstaying a Vietnamese visa results in fines calculated by the day and can complicate future entry — it's genuinely not worth risking for the sake of a few extra days. If your trip is running long, visa extensions and renewals are possible through local immigration offices or authorized travel agencies, generally easier to arrange before your current visa expires rather than after.

"The e-visa system turned what used to be a genuine hassle into a 10-minute online form. Just don't wait until the week before your flight to submit it."

Double-Checking Before You Book

Visa policy details — fees, exemption lists, processing times — change periodically, so always verify current requirements on Vietnam's official immigration portal or your destination country's foreign ministry site rather than relying on older blog posts (including this one) for exact current fees.
