India Tour Packages for Foreign Tourists

Private transport, local guides, and itineraries built around your trip — not a rigid group package. Here's how Zentrevo plans it.

If you're looking at India tour packages from abroad, you've probably already noticed the problem: most options are either rigid group packages built for large numbers of travelers, or generic listings that don't say much about what the trip actually involves. Zentrevo works differently. We help international travelers plan private, customized trips across India — you choose the destinations and pace, we handle the planning, transport, and guides.

This page walks through the packages we build most often, what's included, and what to realistically expect from a first India trip.

Packages Designed for You

"Package" doesn't mean fixed here. Every trip starts with your travel dates, the destinations you're interested in, your preferred pace, accommodation level, and budget — and we build the itinerary from there.

That's a meaningful difference from a standard tour package. Two travelers visiting the same cities can have very different trips depending on how much time they spend in each place, whether they want a guide at every site or just for the highlights, and what kind of hotels they're comfortable with. We plan for foreign travelers specifically, not domestic tourism patterns, which changes things like pacing, food recommendations, and how much local context a guide provides along the way.

Popular India Tour Packages

These are the destinations and combinations we plan most often for international travelers.

01

Golden Triangle Tour — Delhi, Agra, Jaipur

The most common starting point for a first India trip. Delhi covers Mughal and colonial history, Agra is home to the Taj Mahal, and Jaipur offers forts and a well-preserved old city. A focused trip typically runs 5-7 days.

02

Rajasthan Tour — Jaipur, Jodhpur, Udaipur, Jaisalmer

Desert landscapes, lake cities, and towns that each feel distinct — Udaipur's palaces, Jodhpur's blue old city, Jaisalmer's desert fort. Works best as an extension for a longer or repeat trip.

03

Kerala Tour — Backwaters, Beaches, Hills and Culture

Kerala moves at a different pace than North India. Backwater houseboat stays, tea plantations in the hills, and a quieter coastline, often paired with a North India package.

04

Varanasi & Spiritual India

One of the oldest living cities in the world, central to Hindu spiritual practice on the Ganges. The evening river ceremonies suit travelers with real interest in it, rather than a rushed add-on.

05

India Wildlife & Safari

Tiger safaris at parks such as Ranthambore or Bandhavgarh can be added to most North India packages, usually as a two- to three-day extension. Sightings are never guaranteed, but the parks are worth it regardless.

Private & Custom India Tours

Most of the packages above can be booked as private, custom trips rather than joining a scheduled group departure. Here's why foreign travelers tend to prefer that.

Why Travelers Choose Private

  • Flexible schedule — an extra hour somewhere you're enjoying, an earlier start to beat the heat, a change of plan if something comes up
  • Private transportation — your own vehicle with a dedicated driver, rather than a shared coach running on someone else's timetable
  • Local guides based in the regions they take you through, who know the practical details a guidebook won't cover
  • Personalized experiences — food, photography, textiles, architecture, or something more specific, shaped around what you tell us
  • Comfortable travel — less waiting, less shared logistics, more control over pacing on longer intercity drives
  • Support during the trip — a direct contact who already knows your itinerary if plans need to shift

Best India Itinerary for First-Time Visitors

5-7 Days

Enough for the Golden Triangle (Delhi, Agra, Jaipur) without rushing between sites. A solid option if India is one stop on a longer trip, or your vacation time is limited.

8-10 Days

Room to extend into Rajasthan beyond Jaipur, or attach a short Varanasi visit to the Golden Triangle. A common length for a focused first North India trip.

10-14 Days

Enough to combine North and South — Golden Triangle plus Kerala is a popular version — or to slow the pace within North India and add a wildlife safari.

2-3 Weeks

Enough to cover multiple regions properly: North India, a spiritual stop like Varanasi, a wildlife extension, and time in the South. Suited to travelers who want a comprehensive trip rather than an introduction.

If your destination wishlist doesn't comfortably fit your available time, we'll say so and suggest what to prioritize.

Best Time to Visit India

There's no single best season for the whole country — it depends on where you're going.

Timing by Region

  • North India (Delhi, Agra, Jaipur, Rajasthan, Varanasi): October to March is the most comfortable window. April-June gets very hot, and the monsoon (roughly July-September) brings heavy rain in parts of the region
  • Kerala and the South: the monsoon (June-August) is worth avoiding for most itineraries, while October through March remains a reliable window
  • Goa: November through February is peak season, with pleasant weather and full beach and restaurant availability
  • Wildlife safaris: parks are typically open October through June, closing during the monsoon. Feb-April dry conditions often mean better sightings

We'll factor your specific route into timing advice rather than giving a single blanket recommendation.

Is India Easy for Foreign Tourists?

Mostly yes, with some genuine adjustment expected — here's an honest rundown.

Transportation

Traffic is chaotic by most Western standards, and self-driving isn't something we'd recommend for a first visit. A private driver removes this problem almost entirely — you experience the roads from the back seat rather than navigating them yourself.

Culture and Food

India is dense with sensory input — noise, color, crowds, constant activity in cities. Most first-time visitors find the first day or two the most intense, then settle in; a guide for the early part of the trip smooths that adjustment. Food is a highlight for most travelers, but worth easing into — stick to busy, high-turnover restaurants at first, drink bottled or filtered water, and let a guide point you toward street food that's both safe and worth trying.

Language and Crowds

English is widely spoken in hotels, tourist sites, and by guides and drivers across the regions we cover — it's rarely the barrier people expect. Major sites, the Taj Mahal especially, do get busy; timing (early arrivals, avoiding certain days) makes a real difference, and we build that into itinerary planning.

Local Customs

Dress modestly at religious sites, ask before photographing people, and expect some bargaining in markets — a normal, low-stakes part of how transactions work.

The importance of planning is really the deciding factor. India rewards travelers who have some structure and local support in place, and can be genuinely stressful for those winging it without either. Zentrevo's role is largely closing that gap.
