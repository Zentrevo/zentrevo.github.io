How Much Should You Tip in Europe?

It's not a flat 20% like the US, and it's not zero either — a country-by-country breakdown of what's actually expected.

An American traveler once left a 20% tip at a small trattoria in Rome, only to have the waiter jog after her down the street to return the "extra" money, visibly confused about what she was trying to do. She'd applied US tipping instinct to a country where service is already built into the price. It's one of the most common — and most harmless — cultural missteps in European travel, but it's easy to avoid once you know the actual norms.

The General Rule

Service Is Usually Included

Most of continental Europe includes service charge in the listed price (sometimes noted as "servizio incluso" or "service compris"). Tipping on top is appreciated for excellent service but never expected the way it is in the US.

Rounding Up Is the Default

Across most of Western Europe, rounding up the bill or leaving small change is the standard gesture for good service — not a calculated percentage.

Country-by-Country Breakdown

01

France

Service is legally included in restaurant prices. Rounding up a euro or two, or leaving small change, is a nice gesture — not an obligation.

02

Italy

Similar to France — a "coperto" (cover charge) often appears on the bill already. Rounding up is common; a large percentage tip will genuinely confuse most servers.

03

Germany

Rounding up 5-10% is common and appreciated, typically handed directly to the server rather than left on the table.

04

United Kingdom

A service charge (usually 10-12.5%) is often added automatically at restaurants, especially for larger groups — check the bill before tipping again on top.

General Tipping Tips for Europe

  • Check your bill for "service included" or an automatic service charge before tipping extra
  • Taxis: rounding up to the nearest convenient amount is standard almost everywhere in Europe
  • Hotels: a small tip (€1-2 per bag) for porters is appreciated but not obligatory in most countries
  • Tour guides: 5-10 euros per person for a half-day tour is a reasonable, appreciated gesture
  • When in doubt, round up rather than calculating a US-style percentage — it reads as more natural

"Nobody in Europe is quietly judging you for a modest tip. The bigger faux pas is over-tipping and making the moment awkward."

What About Eastern Europe?

Tipping norms in Eastern Europe run closer to 10%, more actively expected than in Western Europe but still well below US standards. If your European city break includes stops in both regions, adjusting expectations country to country avoids both under- and over-tipping.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you tip in restaurants in Europe?

Service is typically included in the bill across most of continental Europe. Rounding up or leaving small change for good service is appreciated but not expected.

How much do you tip taxi drivers in Europe?

Rounding up to the nearest convenient amount is the standard practice in most European countries — a calculated percentage isn't necessary.

Is it rude to over-tip in Europe?

It's not rude, but it can come across as confused or unnecessary, since service is usually already factored into prices. A modest round-up is the more natural gesture.

Do hotels expect tips in Europe?

A small tip for porters (around €1-2 per bag) is appreciated in most countries but not obligatory. Housekeeping tips are less common than in the US.
