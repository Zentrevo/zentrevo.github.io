Does a Schengen Visa Require Travel Insurance?

Yes — and it's one of the few visa requirements that's actually strict. Here's exactly what counts.

Short answer: yes. Travel insurance isn't optional for a Schengen Area visa application — it's a mandatory document, and applications without valid proof of coverage get rejected outright. This is one of the few places where "probably fine" thinking can actually cost you your trip.

What the Coverage Minimum Actually Is

€30,000 Minimum Medical Coverage

Schengen visa insurance must cover at least €30,000 in medical expenses, including emergency hospital treatment and repatriation if something goes seriously wrong. This isn't a suggestion — consulates check the policy documents specifically for this figure.

Coverage for the Entire Trip Duration

The policy needs to cover every single day of your planned stay in the Schengen Area, not just part of it. A gap of even one day between your insurance end date and your visa's validity period can be grounds for rejection.

What Counts as Valid Proof

It Must Name Schengen Countries Specifically

Generic "worldwide" travel insurance sometimes excludes or caps European coverage differently — the policy document needs to explicitly confirm it covers the Schengen Area at the required €30,000 minimum, not just imply broad global coverage.

It Must Be From a Recognized Provider

Some consulates maintain lists of accepted insurers or reject certain budget providers outright. Check your specific destination consulate's requirements page before buying, rather than assuming any policy will be accepted.

Getting This Right Before You Apply

  • Buy insurance only after your travel dates are fairly firm — policies need exact start and end dates
  • Print the policy document showing the €30,000 figure explicitly, not just a generic summary page
  • Double-check coverage includes repatriation, not just hospital treatment
  • Buy insurance covering your entire Schengen trip length, even if you're visiting multiple countries within it
  • Keep a digital copy accessible during your trip — border officials can occasionally ask to see it too

"This is the one visa document nobody should try to wing. Consulates check it line by line, every time."

Does This Apply If You Don't Need a Visa?

If you're from a visa-exempt country and entering the Schengen Area for a short stay, formal proof of insurance usually isn't checked at the border the same way — but it's still strongly recommended regardless. Medical care in Europe can be genuinely expensive without coverage, and it's the same practical advice covered in our broader guide on packing and preparing for a multi-country Europe trip.

Zentrevo can point clients toward Schengen-compliant insurance providers as part of a broader Europe itinerary, so this particular piece of paperwork doesn't become the reason a visa application gets delayed.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much travel insurance coverage does a Schengen visa need?

A minimum of €30,000 in medical coverage, including emergency treatment and repatriation, for the full duration of your planned stay.

Can I use travel insurance from any provider?

It needs to explicitly cover the Schengen Area at the required minimum. Some consulates also maintain specific accepted-provider lists, so check your destination's requirements first.

Do I need Schengen travel insurance if I don't need a visa?

It's not formally required at the border for visa-exempt travelers, but it's still strongly recommended given how expensive uninsured medical care can be in Europe.

What happens if my insurance doesn't meet the minimum?

Your visa application can be rejected outright. Consulates check the coverage figure specifically, so it's worth confirming before you apply, not after.
